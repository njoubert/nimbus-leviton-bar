Sliders that stop on the fives, and a Refresh row that tells you the truth.

Dragging a dimmer now snaps to 5 %, so you land on 35 % rather than 34 %. The knob sits in the notch as you drag; a level set somewhere else is still shown exactly as it is.

The **Refresh** row used to show when the app last fetched the list, which said nothing about how current the lights actually are — the app also keeps a live connection to My Leviton, and a switch flipped at the wall shows up here in about a second. So the row now reports that connection instead: **live** while it is up, and how long ago the list was fetched (`updated 47 seconds ago`) when it is not.

And that word had to be worth something. The connection is pinged every 30 seconds and has ten to answer, so one that has quietly stopped carrying anything — after a sleep, or a change of network — is spotted in seconds rather than minutes. Each minute's fetch is checked against what the connection has been telling us, and a light that changed without it saying so costs it the label and gets it reconnected. It reconnects when your Mac wakes, too, and when you click **Refresh** — which is now the way out of a connection that had given up for the hour.

Underneath all of it the once-a-minute fetch is unchanged, so a connection that dies quietly costs you a little delay, never a wrong reading.

**Open My Leviton on the Web** is gone from the menu.
